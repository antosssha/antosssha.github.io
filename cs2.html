01

The challenge

The central design constraint is simple: the agent should not know what a human could not know. The research focuses on perception uncertainty, reaction delay, partial visibility and behavior under imperfect information.

01No hidden enemy coordinatesTargets must first satisfy a fair-observation layer before they can influence behavior.
02No wallhack-style stateOcclusion matters. Walls and visual blockers can make a target unavailable.
03Human-like uncertaintyReaction time, incomplete visibility and imperfect target acquisition are modeled deliberately.
04Verified ≠ plannedPublic documentation separates working behavior from experiments and future milestones.
This page describes the public research layer only. Private implementation details, datasets, model internals and infrastructure are intentionally excluded.

03

Verified public state

The current v0.7.3 build has a tested set of perception and combat behaviors. These are capabilities verified in controlled development scenarios, not claims of full-match human-level play.

Verified

  • Navigation and basic movement
  • Field-of-view and wall occlusion
  • Vertical visibility
  • 18-part partial-body sensing
  • Human-like reaction delay and noise
  • Smoke, flash and Molotov perception effects
  • Aiming and counter-strafe behavior
  • Cover, peek, retreat and reload states
  • Short-term target memory

In research / next

  • Raw screen-based computer vision
  • Visual enemy / teammate classification
  • More complex multi-target attention
  • HE visual-effect modeling
  • Sound and radar integration
  • Scoped perception behavior
  • Long autonomous full-match validation
04

Current limitations

The project is deliberately explicit about what the present system is not yet doing.

Perception source

The verified agent currently relies on a geometric fair-observation sensor rather than raw image-based computer vision.

Team identity

Enemy / teammate identity is not yet inferred visually. Visual classification remains a future milestone.

Learned components

Some learned movement components are being retrained under the stricter fair-data rules and are not presented as current verified capability.

Benchmark scope

Short controlled test successes are used to validate subsystems. They are not presented as general accuracy or match-performance claims.

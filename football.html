Football Intelligence

01

The problem

A football forecast can look convincing while quietly over-weighting recent form, reputation, a low market price or one attractive statistic. The model is designed to expose those hidden assumptions before a probability is accepted.

01Normalize the oppositionA winning streak against weak opponents is not treated like the same streak against elite opposition.
02Use the actual squadMatch-day squad strength matters more than a team's theoretical strongest XI.
03Challenge the thesisImportant signals must have explicit counter-arguments before the final probability is produced.
04Audit the missAfter the match, the system records which assumptions were over- or under-weighted.
This case study describes methodology and validation discipline. Internal weighting, scoring logic and commercially sensitive implementation details are not published.

03

Decision process

The workflow separates football analysis from market price and freezes the conclusion before the result.

Build the football viewEvaluate form, chance quality, tactics, squad quality, schedule strength, goalkeeper, set pieces and match context before using the price as an anchor.
Run the counter-caseFor every major signal, deliberately search for the argument that could invalidate or weaken it.
Estimate fair probabilityConvert the balanced view into a probability range and compare it with the market's implied probability.
Re-check near kickoffRefresh confirmed lineups, injuries, rotation, weather and price movement before the final decision.
Freeze and auditAfter the match, compare the forecast with actual score, match statistics, xG and market movement instead of rewriting the reasoning.
04

Anti-bias design

The model treats disagreement as useful information. A low price is not automatically “safe,” a favorite is not automatically the most probable outcome, and unavailable data is not invented.

FOR / AGAINST symmetry

Each meaningful factor is recorded on both sides before the final probability is formed.

Upset veto

Before accepting a favorite scenario, the workflow asks whether there is a credible structural route to an upset.

Tail-risk layer

Rare but material events — cards, penalties, early injuries and other match-state shocks — are treated as risk, not predicted as certainties.

Data honesty

If a metric such as PPDA or field tilt cannot be sourced reliably for a match, the framework records that gap instead of fabricating precision.

07

Working-standard evidence

The methodology is backed by a maintained internal working standard. The private document is not published; this section exposes only the safe, high-level rules.

PRIMARY RULE

Football before market

The documented standard requires building the most probable football scenario first. Only after that is a market chosen to represent the scenario.

GoalReduce price anchoring
MANDATORY INPUTS

Multi-factor checklist

Squads, opponent quality, tactics, xG, chance quality, PPDA, field tilt, set pieces, goalkeepers, bench depth, venue, fatigue and motivation are explicitly tracked.

RuleMissing data is a gap, not invented precision
MATH CHECK

Probability control layer

The standard includes implied probability, no-vig normalization, fair odds, edge / EV, Poisson / xG checks and correlation control as validation — not as a substitute for football analysis.

PurposeChallenge the qualitative thesis
Feedback rulePast mistakes are required to feed future estimates instead of being forgotten after the result. This is the core reason the post-match journal exists.
